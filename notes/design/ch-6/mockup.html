When the genome strains

The molecular architecture of cellular stress response is a delicate equilibrium, evolved over eons to withstand thermal fluctuation. However, genetic variations within these chaperone networks—documented extensively in the ClinVar database—suggest a landscape of differential vulnerability.

Plate VI catalogues a subset of these variations, specifically focusing on Heat Shock Proteins (HSPs) and their regulatory elements. These mutations do not simply represent "errors" in the code, but rather distinct structural diversions that may alter the folding efficiency of the entire proteome under hyperthermic conditions.

Clinical significance ≠ individual heat-illness risk prediction. Data provided for academic and archival reference only.

By examining the prevalence of these variants, we can begin to map the theoretical limits of human thermal adaptation at a granular, protein-by-protein level.

CHAPERONE · HSP70 / HSPA1A
Molecular chaperones represent a critical mechanism for the preservation of proteostasis under thermal stress.

The first line of defense

The heat shock protein 70 (HSP70) family, specifically HSPA1A, stands as the central hub of the cellular protein-folding machinery. Operating as an ATP-driven chaperone, it intervenes at the critical moment when a protein begins to lose its native conformation due to rising ambient temperatures.

HSP70 functions by recognizing and binding to exposed hydrophobic patches on damaged or nascent proteins. Under normal conditions, these patches are tucked deep within the protein's core. When thermal energy disrupts these bonds, the chaperone anchors itself to the substrate, preventing the irreversible aggregation that leads to cellular toxicity.

"The binding cycle is strictly regulated by ATP hydrolysis, transitioning the chaperone between a low-affinity ATP-bound state and a high-affinity ADP-bound state to clamp down on its client."

Beyond simple sequestration, HSP70 actively uses the energy from ATP to refold proteins back into their functional, low-energy states. This iterative process is essential for maintaining the proteome's integrity when the cell enters the "danger zone" of 40°C and above.

The expression of HSPA1A is not static; it is rapidly induced by the activation of Heat Shock Factor 1 (HSF1). This transcription factor senses the accumulation of misfolded proteins and triggers a massive upregulation of the chaperone response, effectively flooding the cytoplasm with refolding power.
